WhatsApp reaches customers in the app they already use all day, in a thread they can reply to.
Businesses send through Meta's
[WhatsApp Business Platform](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/get-started),
and OneXtel connects you to it — so the platform's rules are Meta's, and they shape how you plan
every campaign.

## The one rule that shapes everything

Meta separates messages into two kinds, and which one you can send depends entirely on when the
customer last wrote to you.

| | Template message | Session message |
| --- | --- | --- |
| When you can send it | Any time | Only within **24 hours** of the customer's last message |
| Approval | Pre-approved by Meta before first use | None — write it freely |
| Typical use | You start the conversation: an order update, a reminder, an offer | You reply while a conversation is live |

That 24-hour window is Meta's customer service window. Once it closes, a free-form reply will not
send, and you have to fall back to an approved template. Support teams feel this first.

## Message categories

Every template is approved into a category, which decides what it may say and how it is priced.

| Category | For |
| --- | --- |
| [Marketing](/guides/whatsapp/template-categories-marketing) | Offers, launches, re-engagement |
| [Utility](/guides/whatsapp/template-categories-utility) | Updates about something the customer already did — orders, payments, bookings |
| [Authentication](/guides/whatsapp/template-categories-authentication) | One-time passcodes |

<Warning>
**Getting the category wrong is the most common reason a template is rejected.** A message that
reads like an offer will not pass as utility, however it is labelled. See
[Template categories](/guides/whatsapp/template-categories).
</Warning>

## What you can send

Text, images, video, documents and location, with quick-reply buttons and call-to-action buttons
that open a link or dial a number. Customers can reply, and their replies come back to you —
which is the part that makes WhatsApp a conversation rather than a broadcast.

## Before your first message

<Steps>
  <Step title="A WhatsApp Business Account and a number">
    A number that is not already in use on the consumer WhatsApp app, with a display name Meta
    approves. See [Onboarding WhatsApp](/guides/whatsapp/activation).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Approved templates">
    Anything that starts a conversation needs an approved template, so write these before you
    plan the campaign, not after. See
    [Create templates](/api-reference/whatsapp/templates-create).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Opt-in, and a way to opt out">
    Meta requires customers to have agreed to hear from you, and to be able to stop. See
    [Opt in and opt out](/api-reference/whatsapp/opt-in-opt-out).
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
Meta also applies quality ratings and messaging limits per number, based on how customers react
to your messages. Blocks and reports lower your limit; useful, expected messages protect it.
</Note>
