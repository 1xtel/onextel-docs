---
title: "WhatsApp account model"
description: "How Meta's WhatsApp account and Messaging account split works, and what it changes for templates, billing and limits."
---

Meta has split the WhatsApp Business Account (WABA) into two objects: a **WhatsApp account
(WAAC)** for the phone number, and a **Messaging account** for templates and billing. The change
rolled out from 23 September 2026 and reached all businesses by mid-October 2026. Meta moved
every account across automatically.

The split lets one phone number work with several providers at once. Each provider gets its own
Messaging account on the number, while the number itself stays with your business.

A Coexistence number, one you also use in the WhatsApp Business app, is the exception. Meta
converts your existing WhatsApp Business Account into a Messaging account and shares it with the
provider, and creates no provider-specific Messaging account.

Your business is on the new model if Meta Business Suite shows both **WhatsApp accounts** and
**Messaging accounts**.

## The two accounts

| Account | Holds | How many |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **WhatsApp account (WAAC)** | The business phone number, business profile information and product catalogues. Business usernames are planned. | One per phone number. Always owned by your business, and shareable with several providers. |
| **Messaging account** | Message templates, billing and payment method, webhook subscriptions and analytics. | One per provider. Meta creates it when you connect the provider through Embedded Signup. |

Your existing WABA became your provider's Messaging account, with the same ID. The WABA ID you
already use is now the Messaging account ID.

<Note>
Meta's Terms of Service still call the WhatsApp account a "WhatsApp Business Account". To
manage several numbers as a group, Meta links WhatsApp accounts under a primary one; sharing the
primary WhatsApp account with a provider gives that provider every number in the group.
</Note>

## What stays the same

- Cloud API endpoints, phone number IDs and access tokens keep working.
- Your WABA ID keeps working, as the Messaging account ID.
- The new WhatsApp account and Messaging account IDs are additions; they don't replace the IDs
  you store today.

## What changes on a shared number

When more than one provider works on your number:

- **Templates** belong to the Messaging account they were created in. Other providers on the
  number can't see or reuse them, and they aren't copied when you add a provider. See
  [Bring your templates](/guides/whatsapp/add-onextel-to-existing-number#bring-your-templates).
- **Billing** is per Messaging account. Each one has its own payment method, and each provider
  bills through its own Messaging account, whichever provider handles the conversation. A message
  is charged to the Messaging account of the app that sends it.
- **Throughput** is set per phone number, and **messaging limits** (unique users per 24 hours)
  per business portfolio. All providers on the number share both.
- **Incoming messages** go to one provider at a time, chosen by your conversation routing setting.
  See [Conversation routing](/guides/whatsapp/add-onextel-to-existing-number#set-conversation-routing).

<Warning>
If a partner's credit line is attached to a Messaging account that your business owns, that
Messaging account can't be shared with another partner.
</Warning>

## Meta's timeline

| Phase | When | What Meta ships |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. General availability** | From 23 September 2026; all businesses by mid-October 2026 | Accounts migrated automatically. Embedded Signup creates one Messaging account per provider, except on Coexistence numbers. Meta Business Suite shows WhatsApp accounts and Messaging accounts. Meta starts phasing out the two-step verification PIN for eligible numbers, in stages; registering a number still takes a valid 6-digit PIN until Meta announces a change. |
| **2. New Graph API version** | First half of 2027 | The newest Messages API version requires `messaging_account_id` where a sender can use more than one Messaging account on a number. A separate WhatsApp account ID, new endpoints, a new `whatsapp_account` webhook topic and separate permissions. |
| **3. Mandatory transition** | First half of 2028 | Every Messages API version requires `messaging_account_id` in multi-account setups. APIs that take a phone number ID in the path or query use the WhatsApp account ID instead. |

Meta also asks integrations to move from `paid_messaging_account_id` to `messaging_account_id`
by **31 December 2026**. The old field stays as a backward-compatible alias until a later Graph
API version removes it; if a request sends both, `messaging_account_id` wins.

Meta describes the change in
[account model evolution](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/account-model-evolution/).
Meta may update these dates.
